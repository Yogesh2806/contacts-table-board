Step 1 · Design principles

Nielsen's 10 heuristics, as decisions

Each heuristic becomes something you can point at in the interface — and check in the prototype.

01
Visibility of system status
Unsaved dot on the panel. Save enables only when something changed. Column counts update live; the moved card flashes once in its new column.
02
Match the real world
Stages run left to right, Lead to Customer, like a sales pipeline. Plain words: “Save changes”, not “Submit”.
03
User control and freedom
Cancel and Esc drop the draft. Every save shows a toast with Undo. Closing the panel never moves you somewhere new.
04
Consistency and standards
One stage pill, one avatar, one panel — the same in both views. Aa NAD components and tokens throughout.
05
Error prevention
Stage is a fixed list, so it can't be blank or misspelled. Leaving a dirty draft asks Keep editing or Discard.
06
Recognition over recall
The stage picker shows all four options with their colours. View switch shows both names with icons. Shortcuts sit in tooltips.
07
Flexibility and efficiency
Mouse for beginners; ⌘↵, Esc, J/K and arrow keys for experts. Drag-and-drop between columns as a stretch shortcut.
08
Aesthetic, minimal design
Cards carry 4 things: name, role · company, labels, owner. Ink and paper; colour is used only for stages.
09
Recover from errors
Moved the wrong person? Undo in the toast puts the stage and notes back exactly as they were.
10
Help and documentation
The empty Qualified column explains how to fill it. Missing fields say “Add email” instead of staying blank. Press ? for shortcuts.
Lively, within the system Calm motion only where it explains a change: card lifts 2px on hover, panel slides in over 320ms, the moved card fades into its new column, the toast slides up. All of it turns off under reduced motion.
